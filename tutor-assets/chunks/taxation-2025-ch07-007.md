- Example - 7-6

What if: Assume that Jeb (single) sold his stock in Gangbusters Inc., a qualified small business stock, for $200,000 on December 12, 2024. He acquired the stock on November 8, 2016, and his basis in the stock is $50,000. How much tax will he owe on the sale if his ordinary marginal rate is 32 percent? Answer: $0. Because Jeb held the qualified small business stock for more than five years and because he acquired the stock when the exclusion percentage was 100 percent, he will exclude the entire $150,000 gain from income. What if: Assume instead that Jeb sold his Gangbusters stock on December 12, 2024, but had acquired it on February 17, 2021. How much tax will he owe on the sale? Answer: $22,500. Because Jeb didn’t hold the stock for more than five years, he must recognize all $150,000 of long-term capital gain. This gain is taxed at 15 percent, yielding a total tax due of $22,500.

## TAXES IN THE REAL WORLD

The Next Big Thing? NFTs, or non-fungible tokens, are collectible digital assets. They can represent items like images, GIFs, videos, or basically anything that exists online. NFTs are traded on various platforms such as Top Shot, OpenSea, or SuperRare and are typically bought using cryptocurrencies. Their ownership is recorded and tracked on the blockchain. In June 2023, the IRS provided guidance on the taxation of NFTs. This guidance states that the IRS will use a look-through analysis to determine if and NFT is a collectible under §408(m), and the tax treatment will depend on whether the taxpayer is the creator of or an investor in the NFT. For creators, NFTs are treated similar to inventory in that sales will result in ordinary income taxed at ordinary tax rates and subject to self-employment taxes. NFT investors buy and sell NFTs for investment purposes, and most taxpayers would fall into this category. NFTs are often acquired using cryptocurrencies, so purchasing an NFT would trigger a taxable event because the disposal of a cryptocurrency is treated as the sale of property. Upon the sale of the NFT, the taxpayer would have either a short- or long-term capital gain depending on how long the NFT was held. However, when NFTs are classified as collectibles, the maximum long-term capital gain tax rate on these gains is 28 percent, with an additional 3.8 percent net investment tax for high-income taxpayers. The sale of NFTs can be reported on Form 8949 and Schedule D like other capital gains. The IRS is increasing scrutiny of cryptocurrency and digital asset transactions and requires taxpayers to answer the question “At any time during 202X, did you receive, sell, exchange, or otherwise acquire any financial interest in any digital asset?” on the front page of Form 1040. Because most NFT transactions involve cryptocurrency, taxpayers will need to respond “Yes” to this question.

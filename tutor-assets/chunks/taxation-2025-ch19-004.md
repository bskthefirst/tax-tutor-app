What if: Assume that Jakob owns 75 percent of SCR and Ginny owns the remaining 25 percent, and they wanted to bring Al on board as a 20 percent shareholder. Al will transfer appreciated property to SCR in return for stock in SCR valued at $100,000. Will Al defer recognizing any gain realized on the transfer under §351? Answer: No. Al does not control SCR “immediately after” the exchange, taking into account only the stock Al owns in SCR. What if: Suppose Jakob agreed to make an additional property contribution to SCR at the same time as Al’s transfer in order to help Al qualify his transfer under §351. Jakob’s 75 percent ownership interest in SCR was valued at $300,000 at the time of Al’s transfer. Using the IRS standards for rulings, how much property (fair market value) must Jakob contribute to SCR to have his ownership of stock in SCR counted in determining if Al qualifies for deferral under §351?

---

Answer: $30,000. For an accommodation transfer such as this to be respected by the IRS, Jakob must contribute property with a fair market value of 10 percent or more of the fair market value of his existing stock in SCR (10% × $300,000).

Tax Consequences When a Shareholder Receives No Boot When an exchange qualifies under §351, the shareholder’s realized gain or loss is deferred, and this deferral is reflected in the shareholder’s basis in stock received in the exchange. When no boot is involved, the tax basis of stock received in a tax-deferred §351 exchange equals the adjusted tax basis of the property transferred less any liabilities assumed by the corporation.13 The stock is said to have a substituted basis. That is, the adjusted tax basis of the property transferred is substituted for the adjusted tax basis of the property received.14 Exhibit 19-4 provides a template for computing the tax basis of stock received in a tax-deferred §351 transaction.

### Example 19-6

As part of the formation of 360 Air, Al transferred inventory, a building, and land to the corporation in return for 50 percent of the corporation’s stock (50 shares). The property transferred to the corporation had the following fair market values and adjusted tax bases:

| Column 1 | FMV | Adjusted Tax Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inventory | $ 25,000 | $ 15,000 |
| Building | 150,000 | 60,000 |
| Land | 200,000 | 100,000 |
| Total | $375,000 | $175,000 |

In addition, the corporation assumed a mortgage of $75,000 attached to the building and land. The fair market value of the 360 Air stock Al received in the exchange was

---

$300,000. Al realizes a gain of $200,000 on the transfer, computed as follows:

| Column 1 | Column 2 |
| --- | --- |
| Fair market value of 360 Air stock received | $300,000 |
| Plus: Mortgage assumed by 360 Air | +75,000 |
| Amount realized | $375,000 |
| Less: Adjusted tax basis of the property transferred | −175,000 |
| Gain realized | $200,000 |

Assuming Al meets the requirements under §351 to defer recognizing the $200,000 gain realized, what is his tax basis in the 50 shares of 360 Air stock he receives in the exchange? Answer: $100,000. Al’s tax basis in the stock must reflect the gain he defers in the exchange. Al computes tax basis in the 360 Air stock as follows:

| Column 1 | Column 2 |
| --- | --- |
| Adjusted tax basis of property contributed | $175,000 |
| Less: Mortgage assumed by 360 Air | −75,000 |
| Tax basis of 360 Air stock received | $100,000 |

If Al subsequently were to sell his 360 Air stock for its fair market value of $300,000, he would recognize a capital gain of $200,000 ($300,000 − $100,000), an amount equal to the gain he deferred previously.

What if: Assume Al received shares of stock worth $375,000 because 360 Air did not assume the mortgage on the building. What is Al’s tax basis in the shares of 360 Air stock received in the exchange? Answer: $175,000. Al’s tax basis in the stock equals his basis in the contributed assets. Al computes tax basis in the 360 Air stock as follows:

| Column 1 | Column 2 |
| --- | --- |
| Adjusted tax basis of property contributed | $175,000 |
| Less: Mortgage assumed by 360 Air | −0 |
| Tax basis of 360 Air stock received | $175,000 |

Note that if Al subsequently were to sell his 360 Air stock for its fair market value of $375,000, he would recognize a capital gain of $200,000 ($375,000 − $175,000)

What if: Assume Al did not meet the requirements under §351 and was required to recognize the $200,000 gain realized. What is Al’s tax basis in the 50 shares of 360 Air stock received in the exchange? Answer: $300,000. Al’s tax basis in his stock equals its fair market value. If Al subsequently sells his 360 Air stock for $300,000, he will not recognize any further gain.

Tax Consequences When a Shareholder Receives Boot We calculate the amount of gain recognized when boot is received in a §351 transaction by allocating the boot received to each contributed property using the relative fair market values of the properties.15 The amount of gain recognized with respect to each asset is the lesser of the gain realized or the boot allocated to such asset. The character of the recognized gain (i.e., capital gain, §1231 gain, ordinary income) is determined by the character of each contributed asset.

Distributions from Traditional Defined Contribution Plans

---

When employees receive distributions (i.e., withdraw funds) from traditional defined contribution plans, the distributions are taxed as ordinary income. However, when employees receive distributions from these plans either too early or too late, they must pay a penalty in addition to the income taxes they owe on the distributions. Generally, employees who receive distributions before they reach 59½ years of age or 55 years of age and have separated from service (retired or let go by employer)

are subject to a 10 percent nondeductible penalty on the amount of the early distributions.13

### Example 13-7

What if: Assume that when she reaches 60 years of age, Carla Perez retires from CBA and receives a $60,000 distribution from her traditional 401(k) account in the year she retires. Assuming her marginal ordinary tax rate is 32 percent, what amount of tax and penalty will Carla pay on the distribution? Answer: $19,200 taxes ($60,000 × 32%) + $0 penalty (she is over 59½ years of age on date of distribution). What if: Assume that when Carla is 57 years of age and still employed by CBA, she requests and receives a $60,000 distribution from her traditional 401(k) account. What amount of tax and penalty is Carla required to pay on the distribution? Answer: $19,200 taxes ($60,000 × 32%) + $6,000 penalty ($60,000 × 10%).

What if: Assume that Carla’s employment is terminated by CBA when she is 57 years old. In that same year, Carla requests and receives a $60,000 distribution from her traditional 401(k) account. What amount of tax and penalty is Carla required to pay on the distribution? Answer: $19,200 taxes ($60,000 × 32%) + $0 penalty (she is over 55 years of age and she no longer works for CBA).

## THE KEY FACTS

### Traditional Defined Contribution Plans: Distributions

Taxed at ordinary rates.

10 percent penalty on early distributions:

Before 59½ years of age if not retired or

Before 55 years of age if retired or let go.

Required minimum distributions:

Must begin receiving by April 1 of the later of (1) the year after the year in which taxpayer reaches the

---

applicable age or (2) the year after the year in which the employee retires.

Must receive required minimum distributions each year after retirement year.

25 percent penalty to the extent required minimum distributions are not made.

Taxpayers who fail to receive a required minimum distribution for (pertaining to) a particular year are also penalized. The year for which taxpayers must receive their first required minimum distribution is the later of: The year in which the employee reaches the applicable age or The year in which the employee retires.

The applicable age is currently 73.14 Taxpayers must receive their first required minimum distribution no later than April 1 of the year after the year to which the distribution pertains.15 Taxpayers generally must receive required minimum distributions for subsequent years by the end of the year to which they pertain. Thus, a taxpayer who turns 73 years of age in 2023 must receive their first required minimum distribution (for 2023) by April 1, 2024 (April 1 of the year after they reach the applicable age of 73). The same taxpayer must also receive a required minimum distribution for 2024 by December 31, 2024. The amount of the required minimum distribution for a particular year is the taxpayer’s account balance at the end of the year prior to the year to which the distribution pertains, multiplied by a percentage from a life expectancy table called the “Uniform Lifetime Table.” The percentage is based on the distribution period (in years), which depends on the taxpayer’s age at the end of the year to which the distribution pertains. What if: Suppose that Jason retires from CBA in 2024 at age 74 (and he is still 74 at year-end). When must he receive his required minimum distribution for 2024 from his traditional 401(k) account? Answer: By April 1, 2025.

What if: Assuming Jason retires in 2024 and that his traditional 401(k) account balance on December 31, 2023, was $3,500,000, what is the amount of the required minimum distribution Jason must receive by April 1, 2025? Answer: $137,200($3,500,000 × 3.92%). The percentage from the Uniform Life Table for determining the required minimum distribution is based on Jason’s age at the end of 2024, which is 74 years old. What if: Assuming Jason’s traditional 401(k) account balance on December 31, 2024, is $3,700,000, what is the amount of the required minimum distribution for 2025 that Jason must receive sometime during 2025 (in addition to the $137,200 distribution by April 1)? The applicable percentage for a 75-year-old in 2025 is 4.07 percent. Answer: $150,590($3,700,000 × 4.07%) must be received by Jason during 2025.

The consequences for failing to receive timely required minimum distributions from defined contribution plans are even more severe than for receiving distributions too early. Taxpayers incur a 25 percent nondeductible penalty on the amount of a required minimum distribution (RMD) they should have received but did not (the penalty is payable as an additional tax for the calendar year containing the last day on which the amount is required to be distributed).16 The taxpayer reports this additional tax on Form 5329, “Additional Taxes on Qualified Plans (Including IRAs) and Other Tax- Favored Accounts,” part IX. It is vital that payouts from defined contribution plans be monitored to avoid not only the 10 percent premature distribution penalty but also the 25 percent penalty for failing to receive timely required minimum distributions.

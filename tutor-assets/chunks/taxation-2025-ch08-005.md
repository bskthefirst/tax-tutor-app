The major itemized deductions that are deductible for both regular tax and AMT purposes using the same limitations are: Charitable contributions. Home mortgage interest expenses. Gambling losses.

Some deductions are deductible for regular tax and AMT purposes but have different limitations. For example, interest income that is tax exempt for regular tax purposes but included in the AMT base is included in investment income for determining the AMT investment interest expense deduction.

AMT Exemption To help ensure that most taxpayers aren’t required to pay the alternative minimum tax, Congress allows taxpayers to deduct an alternative minimum tax (AMT) exemption amount to determine their alternative minimum tax base.8 The amount of the exemption depends on the taxpayer’s filing status. The exemption is phased out (reduced) by 25 cents for every dollar the AMTI exceeds the threshold amount. Exhibit 8-5 identifies, by filing status, the base exemption amount, the phase-out threshold, and the range of AMTI over which the exemption is phased out for 2024.9

Tentative Minimum Tax and AMT Computation Taxpayers compute the tentative minimum tax by multiplying the AMT base by the applicable AMT rates. The 2024 AMT tax rate schedule consists of the following two brackets: 26 percent on the first $232,600 (indexed for inflation annually) of AMT base for all taxpayers other than married taxpayers filing separately ($116,300, indexed for inflation annually, for married taxpayers filing separately). 28 percent on AMT base in excess of $232,600 (indexed for inflation annually) for all taxpayers other than married taxpayers filing separately ($116,300, indexed for inflation annually, for married taxpayers filing separately).

Exhibit 12-3 Text Alternative (Chapter 12) Return to Exhibit 12-3 The economic and tax consequences of Julie’s initial stock option award depend on the future share price of TPI, at the time Julie decides to exercise her options, and at the time Julie sells the shares received from the option exercise. The stock option examples in this chapter initially draw from the timeline below for the necessary assumptions regarding these key variables. Year 1: Julie receives stock option award on the grant date: January 1, Year 1. TPI trades at 5 dollars per share. This is also the exercise price of the options. Year 3: Julie can exercise her options on the vesting date: January 1, Year 3: if she chooses. TPI trades at 15 dollars per share. Year 4: Julie exercises all her vested options (both NQOs and ISOs) on the exercise date: December 31, Year 3. (Julie will acquire 20,000 shares of TPI (200 NQOs times 100 shares per NQO) with NQOs and 5,000 shares of TPI (50 ISOs times 100 shares per ISO) with ISOs. In total, Julie will acquire 25,000 shares of TPI. TPI will receive 125,000 dollars from Julie when she exercises her options. If new shares are issued, however, TPI’s earnings per share will drop relative to what it would have been had the new shares not been issued. If, to avoid dilution of its earnings per share, TPI purchases its own shares from the market instead of issuing new shares, it would pay 500,000 dollars (25,000 shares purchased times 20 dollars per share), resulting in total cash outflows of 375,000 dollars net of the 125,000 dollars Julie paid in to exercise her options). TPI trades at 20 dollars per share. Year 6: Julie sells all the shares she received from the stock option exercise on December 31, Year 5. TPI trades at 25 dollars per share. After Year 10: Julie’s options, if she had held them, would have expired on the expiration date.

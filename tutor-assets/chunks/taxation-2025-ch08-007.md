### Employee FICA Taxes Payable

## THE KEY FACTS

### Employee FICA Tax

Tax base is employee salary or wages.

Social Security tax

---

6.2 percent rate on tax base.

2024 tax base limit is $168,600.

Medicare tax

1.45 percent rate on tax base.

Tax base unlimited.

Additional Medicare tax

.9 percent rate on tax base in excess of $200,000 ($125,000 for married filing separately; $250,000 for married filing jointly).

Both employees and employers have to pay FICA taxes on employee salary, wages, and other compensation paid by employers. The Social Security tax rate for employees is 6.2 percent of their salary or wages (tax base limited to $168,600 in 2024), the Medicare tax rate for employees is 1.45 percent of their salary or wages, and the additional Medicare tax rate is .9 percent on salary or wages in excess of $200,000 ($125,000 for married filing separately; $250,000 of combined salary or wages for married filing jointly). Employers withhold the employees’ FICA tax liabilities from the employees’ paychecks for both the Social Security tax and the Medicare tax. For the additional Medicare tax, employers are required to withhold the tax at a rate of .9 percent for any salary or wages above $200,000, irrespective of the taxpayer’s filing status (e.g., single, married filing separately, married filing jointly, or head of household).16 Taxpayers use Form 8959, Additional Medicare Tax, to determine their liability for the additional Medicare tax and report all of the additional Medicare tax withheld as a tax payment on Form 1040, Schedule 2, line 11. Employers also must pay their portion of the Social Security tax (6.2 percent of employee salary or wages) and Medicare tax (1.45 percent of employee salary or wages, regardless of the amount of salary or wages). In contrast to employees, employers are not subject to the additional Medicare tax on employee salary or wages.

What amount of FICA taxes for the year must EWD pay on Courtney’s behalf? Answer: $13,643, which includes $10,453 of Social Security tax ($168,600 × 6.2%) and $3,190 of Medicare tax ($220,000 × 1.45%). Employers do not pay the additional Medicare tax.

Employees who work for multiple employers within a calendar year may receive aggregate compensation that exceeds the Social Security tax base but does not exceed it with any employer. Because each employer is required to withhold Social Security taxes on the employee’s wages until the employee has reached the tax base limit with that employer, the employee may end up paying Social Security tax in excess of the required maximum. As it does in situations in which excess Medicare tax has been withheld, the IRS treats the excess Social Security tax paid through withholding as an additional federal income tax payment (or credit) on Schedule 3, line 11 and page 2, line 31 of Form 1040. The government refunds the excess withholding to the employee through either lower taxes payable with the tax return or a larger tax refund. Employers, on the other hand, are not able to recover excess Social Security taxes paid on behalf of their employees.

- Example - 25-11

What if: Suppose that Lucas made a taxable gift of $8 million in 2011 (when the exemption was $5 million) and made current taxable gifts of $20 million. In this example, Lucas has cumulative transfers of $28 million, and because he used $5 million of exemption in 2011, he has an unused exemption equivalent of $8.61 million ($13.61 million minus $5 million). The unused applicable credit is the difference between the tax on the current $13.61 million exemption ($5,389,800) and the tax on the $5 million exemption equivalent previously used ($1,945,800) or $3,444,000 ($5,389,800 − $1,945,8000). The gift tax due is calculated below using the

tax formula in Exhibit 25-3:

The gift tax formula requires donors to keep track of the unused applicable credit to calculate the tax due. Fortunately, there is a shortcut method for calculating the gift tax when cumulative taxable transfers exceed $1 million. Rather than track the tax on the exemption, instead subtract gifts that were in excess of the exemption at the time of the gift and the current exemption from the cumulative taxable transfers to calculate the taxable transfers in the current period. In this example, $5 million of the prior $8 million gift was offset by the 2011 exemption. Hence, Lucas would have only paid gift tax on $3 million of the $8 million gift. If the cumulative transfers exceed $1 million, the tax is computed using a flat 40% tax rate. Here’s how the shortcut works in this example:

| Description | Exemption Equivalent |
| --- | --- |
| Cumulative taxable transfers ($8 million plus $20 million) | $28,000,000 |
| Less gifts subject to tax in prior periods | −3,000,000 |
| Less current exemption | −13,610,000 |
| Taxable transfers in this period | $11,390,000 |
| Times marginal tax rate (40%) | × 40% |
| Gift tax on current gifts at the current tax rate | $ 4,556,000 |

To recap, rather than track the tax on the exemption, subtract the amount of gifts from prior periods that were in excess of exemption at that time and the current exemption from total taxable transfers. As long as taxable transfers exceed $1 million, the tax is calculated by multiplying the difference by the marginal tax rate, 40 percent.

## THE FEDERAL ESTATE TAX

### LO 25-3

The estate tax is designed to tax the value of property owned or controlled by an individual at death, the decedent. Because federal gift and estate taxes are integrated, taxable gifts affect the tax base for the estate tax. Exhibit 25-6 presents the estate tax formula.

Suppose you asked your favorite AI query tool the following question: “What basis does a shareholder take in property received in a liquidating distribution?” and the AI tool responded as follows: When a shareholder receives property in a liquidating distribution from a corporation, the shareholder’s basis in the property received is generally determined by the fair market value of the property on the date of distribution. The basis is important because it is used to calculate potential capital gains or losses when the shareholder later disposes of the property.

Here’s how the basis in property received in a liquidating distribution is typically determined: 1. Fair Market Value (FMV) Basis: The starting point for determining the shareholder’s basis in the property is its fair market value (FMV) on the date of the liquidating distribution.

This FMV is usually the value of the property in an arm’s length transaction between a willing buyer and a willing seller. 2. Basis Allocation: If the liquidating distribution consists of multiple properties or assets, the total FMV is allocated among the various assets received. Each asset’s basis is determined based on its allocated portion of the total FMV. 3. Adjusted Basis: In some cases, the basis in the property may be subject to certain adjustments. For example, if the shareholder receives property subject to debt or liabilities, the basis of the property may be adjusted to reflect the assumed liabilities. Additionally, any cash received in the liquidating distribution may be considered as part of the basis. 4. Reduction for Prior Distributions: If the shareholder has previously received liquidating distributions from the same corporation, the basis in the property received in the current distribution is generally reduced by the amount of any prior distributions that were treated as a return of capital.

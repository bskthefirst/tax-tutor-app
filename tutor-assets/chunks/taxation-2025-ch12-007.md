## THE KEY FACTS

### Incentive Stock Options (ISOs)

No tax consequences on grant date.

No tax consequences on vesting date.

No regular tax consequences on exercise date if employee holds stock for two years from grant date and one year from exercise date.

If holding requirements are not met (if there is a disqualifying disposition), the options become an NQO.

For AMT, the bargain element is included in AMT income on the exercise date.

When employee sells stock, employee recognizes long-term capital gain or loss on difference between selling price and exercise price.

Employers receive no deduction unless employee doesn’t meet holding period requirements.

## TAXES IN THE REAL WORLD

Stock Options Elon Musk co-founded Tesla in 2003. In 2010, Tesla went public. Fast forward to 2012: Tesla was building cars by hand and producing only about five cars a week. Needless to say, Tesla was struggling and its stock price was $6.06 per share when the market closed on August 12, 2012. To create incentives for Elon to grow the company, Tesla’s Board of Directors created a nonqualified stock option (NQO) plan on August 13, 2012, with 10 milestones. The NQO plan granted Elon the right to exercise or acquire 26,374,505 Tesla shares at an exercise price of $6.24 per share (both the number of shares and exercise price have been adjusted for a 5-1 stock split occurring on August 31, 2020). The plan indicated that the options vest in 10 equal installments based on

Tesla’s attainment of operational and market capitalization milestones, and that these NQOs expire on August 13, 2022. Elon did what many founders do, he held onto most of the options even as the Page 12-14 stock price rose. Tesla’s 2021 proxy statement disclosed that in October of 2021 Elon still held NQOs to exercise 22,862,050 more Tesla shares at the $6.24 exercise price and that he must exercise (use) them or lose them by August 13, 2022. The price on that date was approximately $900 ($300 times the 3 for 1 split occurring on 8/25/2022). The problem is that each time Elon exercises a NQO to acquire a share, he must pay Tesla the $6.24 exercise price and pay the U.S. Treasury $377 in income taxes (see how the $377 tax liability is calculated below). When a NQO is exercised, the taxpayer must include the value of the bargain element (the difference between the current market price of the stock and the exercise price) into taxable income. For each share acquired through the NQO exercise, Elon will include the $1,019.60 ($1,025.84 market price − $6.24 exercise price) bargain element into taxable income and pay taxes of $377 ($1,019.60 bargain element × 37% the highest marginal tax rate). Assuming he exercises all the NQOs, Elon will pay Tesla $143 million ($6.24 × 22,862,050 shares) for the exercised shares. He will also recognize $23.6 billion (22,862,050 shares × $1,019.60 bargain element) in taxable income and pay $8.6 billion ($23.6 billion × 37%) more in federal income tax.

Even though Elon owned a staggering 177,032,165 Tesla shares (based on his December 28, 2021, SEC filing) and had a net worth of $227 billion—making him the world’s richest person (as of December 31, 2022)—all that wealth isn’t liquid. Elon has said that he would prefer to keep all of his Tesla shares—stating that he was the first to put money into Tesla, and he wants to be the last one to take money out. But without selling shares, it would be difficult and very likely he would not have enough cash to pay Tesla the $143 million for NQO exercises to acquire the shares and still be able to pay the IRS $8.6 billion in taxes on the income from the NQO exercise. Elon’s strategy became clear during November and December of 2021, when he filed 11 reports with the SEC disclosing that all of the remaining 2012 NQOs were exercised to acquire 22,862,050 Tesla shares. Additionally, the SEC disclosures reveal that Elon sold 9,880,662 Tesla shares using same-day sales for $10.1 billion, which provided him with enough cash to pay for both the NQO exercises and taxes. The NQOs exercised, the Tesla market price, the NQO exercise price, and the same-day sale prices were all obtained from Elon’s SEC filings, which also noted that “the shares of common stock were sold solely to satisfy the reporting person’s tax withholding obligations related to the exercise of stock options.” In 2018, Tesla’s Board of Directors granted Elon another set of stock options with rights to purchase 33,773,400 more Tesla shares, which vest to Elon as Tesla reaches 12 additional operational and market capitalization milestones. Employer Tax Considerations for Stock Options As is true for employees, an employer’s tax treatment of stock options depends on whether the options are NQOs or ISOs.

Nonqualified options With NQOs, employers deduct the bargain element that employees recognize as compensation or ordinary income when the employees exercise NQOs.22 From the employer’s perspective, no other date is relevant for tax purposes.

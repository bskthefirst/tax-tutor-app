Businesses incur costs called organizational expenditures when organizing or forming a new business entity. These expenses typically include legal and filing fees, costs associated with creating the entity’s structure, and other related organizational costs. Organizational expenditures are considered capital expenditures and, therefore, are not immediately deductible in the year they are incurred. Rather these expenditures are capitalized. Taxpayers may amortize the expenditures over 180 months on a straight-line basis starting in the month the business begins is operations. If the organizational expenditures are below a threshold amount of $50,000, taxpayers can elect to immediately deduct a limited amount (up to $5,000) of the costs in the first year of business operations. However, this deduction is reduced by the amount of organizational expenditures exceeding $50,000. Since tax laws change and a taxpayer’s circumstances can affect the treatment of organizational expenditures, it is important to consult with a tax professional or review the most current tax regulations for any updates or changes in the tax treatment of organizational expenditures.

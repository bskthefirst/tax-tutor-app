The corporation does not recognize gain on the distribution of its own stock in exchange for property in a reorganization.

The stock received in a reorganization has a tax basis equal to the tax basis of the stock surrendered in the exchange (substituted basis) increased by any gain recognized and reduced by cash or boot received.

The assets transferred to the acquiring corporation in a reorganization carry over the tax basis of the shareholders contributing the property (carryover basis).

- LO 19-5: Calculate the tax consequences that apply to the parties to a complete liquidation of a corporation. In general, shareholders receiving a distribution in complete liquidation of a corporation recognize gain and (usually) loss in the exchange.

Corporations owning 80 percent or more (vote power and value) of the liquidating corporation do not recognize gain or loss on receipt of liquidating distributions.

---

The liquidating corporation recognizes gain and (usually) loss on the distribution of property to those shareholders who are taxable on the distribution.

The liquidating corporation cannot deduct losses on property distributed to a related person if the distribution of loss property is non–pro rata or the loss property is disqualified property (property was previously contributed to the corporation in a §351 transaction whose principal purpose was to avoid tax).

The liquidating corporation does not recognize gain or loss on the distribution of property to a corporate shareholder owning 80 percent or more of the stock and cannot deduct losses on distributions to other (minority) shareholders.

In general, the tax basis of each asset received by the shareholder in a taxable complete liquidation equals the asset’s fair market value on the date of the distribution.

The adjusted tax basis of each asset received by a corporate shareholder (owning 80 percent or more of the stock) in a tax-deferred complete liquidation carries over from the liquidating corporation.

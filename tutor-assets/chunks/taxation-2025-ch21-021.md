Inventory: If a partnership holds inventory for sale to customers, any gain from the sale of this inventory is generally treated as ordinary income. 2. Depreciable Business Property: If the partnership owns depreciable business property (e.g., machinery, equipment, vehicles) and sells or exchanges it at a gain, the portion of the gain attributable to depreciation may be treated as ordinary income. 3. Gain from the sale or exchange of these unrealized receivables is treated as ordinary income. 4. Goodwill and Other Section 197 Intangibles: If the partnership owns goodwill or other intangible assets amortizable under Section 197 of the Internal Revenue Code, gain from the sale of these assets may be treated as ordinary income. It’s important to note that the treatment of hot assets can impact the character of income recognized by a partner upon the sale of their partnership interest. When a partner sells their interest, they may be allocated a share of the partnership’s gain or loss, including any gain attributable to hot assets. This income may be subject to ordinary income tax rates rather than the typically lower capital gains rates. The specific rules regarding hot assets can be complex, and tax laws are subject to change. Partnerships and their partners should consult with a tax professional to ensure proper tax treatment and compliance with current tax laws.

## THE KEY FACTS

### Effect of Distributions on E&P

E&P is reduced by distributions as follows:

Cash distributed.

E&P adjusted tax basis of noncash depreciated property (fair market value less than or equal to its E&P adjusted tax basis).

Fair market value of noncash appreciated property (fair market value greater than its E&P adjusted tax basis).

Noncash property distributions are reduced by any liabilities assumed by the shareholder on property received.

E&P reductions for distributions cannot cause E&P to drop below zero.

Current E&P For most types of property, the corporation’s E&P basis is the same as the corporation’s taxable income basis. In these situations, no special adjustment to current E&P is necessary. If the distributed property is appreciated (fair market value of property distributed is greater than the property’s adjusted tax basis), the resulting gain is included in taxable income and increases the tax paid (or payable). The taxes paid or payable on the distribution is part of the negative adjustment to current E&P for all the federal taxes the corporation paid for that year. Thus, no separate adjustment to current E&P is necessary for the taxes paid or payable on the distribution. If the fair market value of the distributed property is less than the property’s adjusted tax basis, the loss is not deductible in computing taxable income. However, current E&P must be adjusted if the E&P basis in the distributed property differs from the corporation’s taxable income basis and the distributed property is appreciated (fair market value of property distributed in excess of the property’s E&P adjusted tax basis). That is, current E&P must reflect the E&P gain on distributions of appreciated property (E&P losses on property distributions can be ignored because they are not recognized). The adjustment is necessary because taxable income includes the taxable gain (not the E&P gain).

Accumulated E&P To reflect that a distribution reduces a corporation’s economic resources to pay dividends in future years, a corporation reduces its accumulated E&P at the end of the current year. The amount of the reduction depends on whether the distributed property was appreciated or depreciated for E&P purposes. When a corporation distributes appreciated property (fair market value in excess of E&P adjusted tax basis), the distribution reduces accumulated E&P at year-end by the fair market value of the property distributed. However, when a corporation distributes depreciated property (E&P adjusted tax basis in excess of fair market value of the property), the distribution reduces accumulated E&P at the end of the year by the E&P adjusted tax basis of the property.14 When the distributed property is subject to a liability, the amount of the liability assumed by the shareholder(s) increases accumulated E&P, essentially netting the liability against the distribution of
